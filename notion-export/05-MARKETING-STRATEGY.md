## 1. Executive Summary

EGO LTD sells one thing that London's property market is desperate for and rarely gets: **reliability**. A single number for repairs, planned upkeep, and the small jobs nobody else will touch — with fixed prices agreed in writing, insured tradespeople who turn up in the window and tidy up, and a custom field-service app that gives every customer a live job-tracking link, an itemised invoice, and a per-property audit trail.

That last point is the wedge. Most of EGO's competitors — sole traders, handyman apps, scattered subcontractors — cannot produce a property-level maintenance record, a reporting trail, or proof of a fixed price agreed up front. EGO can. That turns "trust me" into "here's the receipt", and it is exactly the proof that landlords, letting agents, and commercial facilities managers need before they hand over a portfolio.

**The strategic call:** do not chase one-off homeowner jobs as the growth engine. Win them, but treat them as proof and cashflow. The real prize is **recurring, contracted revenue from landlords, letting/managing agents, and commercial/facilities clients** — buyers who have many properties, repeat needs, and a budget line for maintenance. One agent relationship can be worth more than fifty homeowner call-outs, and it compounds.

**12-month objective:**

- Reach a steady **40–60 qualified leads per month** by month 12 (from a near-standing start).
- Sign **6–10 recurring B2B accounts** (agents / landlords with 5+ properties, or commercial planned-maintenance contracts).
- Get to **30%+ of monthly revenue from recurring/contracted work** by month 12.
- Build a public reputation moat: **50+ genuine reviews** across Google and the relevant trade directories, and a **top-3 Google Map Pack position** for the priority "[trade] + South-East London" searches.

This is a 12-month plan to go from "a good local firm" to "the default maintenance partner for SE London property", funded on a realistic small-business budget of roughly **£1,500–£2,500/month** ramping over the year.

## 2. Market & Competitor Landscape

London's maintenance and property-care market is enormous, fragmented, and built on broken trust. Demand is structural: an ageing housing stock, a huge private rental sector, strict landlord compliance obligations, and a commercial estate that cannot tolerate downtime. The problem is never "is there work" — it is "who can I actually rely on".

**Who EGO competes with, and where EGO wins:**

| Competitor type | What they do well | Where they fall short | EGO's wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole traders / "a bloke I know"** | Cheap, personal, flexible | No insurance proof, no-shows, cash jobs, no record, vanish when busy | Insured, fixed written price, turn up in the window, full digital record |
| **Handyman apps / aggregators** (Fantastic, Aspect, TaskRabbit-style) | Slick booking, brand recognition | Faceless, churn of strangers per visit, opaque pricing, no relationship, no per-property continuity | One accountable team, clear up-front pricing, a relationship that remembers your property |
| **Big FM / facilities firms** | Scale, compliance, account management | Expensive, slow, won't do small jobs, minimum contract sizes, London SME gets ignored | Enterprise-grade record-keeping and reporting **at SME speed and price**, will do the small job |
| **Single-trade contractors** (just a painter, just a plumber) | Deep specialism | You have to project-manage 4 firms for one turnaround | One number for the whole job — carpentry, paint, tiling, fitting, grounds |

**The honest competitive truth:** EGO is not the cheapest, and shouldn't pretend to be. EGO wins on **trust, breadth, and proof**. The market is crowded with people who *say* they're reliable. EGO can *show* it — a written price before work starts, a live tracking link during, an itemised invoice and property record after. That is a genuine, defensible difference, and the messaging should lean on it hard.

**The gap in the market:** the under-served middle. Buyers who are too small for a big FM contract but too professional to risk a cash-in-hand sole trader. Landlords with 3–20 units. Independent letting agents. A café chain with eight sites. A block manager juggling reactive repairs. **That middle is EGO's home turf.**

## 4. Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs) & Priority Ranking

The single most important strategic decision in this plan is **what to attack first**. Get this wrong and you spend a year doing low-value one-off jobs at the mercy of seasonality and price-shoppers. Get it right and you build a recurring revenue base that compounds.

### The ranking, and the argument for it

**Priority 1 — Letting & managing agents (highest leverage).**
One relationship unlocks dozens of properties and a continuous flow of repairs, voids, and turnarounds. The buyer is professional, makes repeat decisions, and values reliability over the lowest price because *their* reputation is on the line with the landlord. EGO's job-tracking link and per-property audit trail are tailor-made for this buyer — they solve the agent's real problem (looking accountable to landlords). **Attack first.** It's a slower sale (relationship + trust), so start now so it matures by month 4–6.

**Priority 2 — Landlords (3–20 units).**
Recurring need, real compliance pain, and they hate voids costing them rent. Reachable directly and through the same agent channel. High lifetime value, repeat work, and natural cross-sell into planned-maintenance care plans. **Attack in parallel with agents** — much of the messaging and the proof points overlap.

**Priority 3 — Commercial / facilities (independent operators, multi-site SMEs).**
The highest-value individual contracts (planned maintenance with reporting), but the longest sales cycle and the most procurement friction. The custom app's planned-maintenance scheduling and reporting are strong here. **Build pipeline from month 2, expect to close from month 4–9.** Don't make it the month-1 focus — it won't pay the bills fast enough.

**Priority 4 — Homeowners (one-off jobs).**
Highest volume, fastest to win via local SEO and ads, lowest lifetime value, most price-sensitive, most seasonal. **They are not the growth engine — they are the cashflow engine and the proof factory.** Every happy homeowner is a review, a referral, and a candidate for a care plan. Win them efficiently through "near me" search and reviews, but don't pour the budget here expecting it to build the business.

**In one line:** *Pursue agents and landlords for the recurring revenue that builds the company; service homeowners efficiently for cashflow, reviews, and referrals; develop commercial as the high-value long game.*

## 5. Brand & Messaging

**Brand voice:** plain, direct, honest. No jargon, no overpromising. Never publish a claim EGO can't back up. This is not a constraint — it *is* the brand. In a market full of inflated promises, restraint reads as confidence.

### Messaging pillars (and the proof behind each)

1. **One number for everything.** Repairs, planned upkeep, the small jobs. *Proof: the full service list — carpentry, paint, plumbing, tiling, exterior, fitting, turnarounds, planned maintenance.*
2. **A price agreed in writing, up front.** No surprises. *Proof: written quotes; materials charged at cost; clear call-out/hourly/half-day/full-day rates.*
3. **We turn up, and we tidy up.** Reliability is the product. *Proof: agreed time windows; reviews; the live job-tracking link customers can watch.*
4. **Fully insured, and honest about our limits.** *Proof: insurance; gas/electrical done by certified specialists EGO arranges — said openly, not hidden.*
5. **You get a record, not just a job.** *Proof: itemised invoices, per-property audit trail, planned-maintenance reporting from the custom app.*

### Tone — do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Say exactly what's included and what it costs | Use "from £X" bait pricing or hidden extras |
| Admit limits ("gas and electrical we arrange with certified specialists") | Imply EGO does everything in-house |
| Use real photos, real reviews, real job examples | Use stock-photo perfection or invented testimonials |
| Speak like a tradesperson who respects your time | Use corporate facilities-management jargon |
| Quantify only what's true | Publish a stat or claim you can't evidence |

### Tagline system

- **Master line:** *The jobs that need doing, done properly.*
- **Homeowner:** *The job done right, the price agreed first.*
- **Landlord/agent:** *Reliable maintenance across every property — with a record to prove it.*
- **Commercial:** *Planned maintenance that prevents the emergency.*

### Objection-handling lines

- *"You're not the cheapest."* → "No. We're the firm that turns up, finishes the job, and gives you a price in writing first. Cheap quotes that drift are what cost you."
- *"Can you do gas/electrical?"* → "We arrange certified specialists for gas and electrical — fully compliant, all coordinated through us, so you still only deal with one number."
- *"How do I know the price won't change?"* → "Because you agree it in writing before we start. Materials are charged at cost, with nothing hidden."
- *"I've been let down before."* → "That's most of why our customers come to us. You'll get a live tracking link for the job and an agreed window — so you're never left wondering."

## 6. Channel Strategy (priority + rough budget)

Channels are ordered by leverage for EGO specifically. Spend follows the priority ICPs: most effort into the B2B recurring channel and the local-search/reviews foundation; paid spend kept lean and accountable.

### 6.1 Local SEO + Google Business Profile — **foundation, do first**
For a local trade, the Google Map Pack and "[trade] near me / in [area]" searches are the single biggest source of inbound homeowner and landlord leads. EGO already has service+area pages (Lewisham SE13, Greenwich SE10, Deptford SE8, plus North/East London pages) — that's the SEO skeleton; now activate it.
- **Actions:** Fully complete and verify the **Google Business Profile** (categories, service list, service areas, hours, real photos, Q&A). Post weekly (a finished job, a tip). Add **LocalBusiness schema** to every service+area page. Make sure every area page has unique, genuinely local copy (not spun duplicates — Google penalises that, and it reads cheap).
- **Reviews engine:** wire the field-service app to **automatically request a review at job completion** via the email campaigns tool, with a one-tap link to Google. This is the highest-ROI activity in the whole plan.
- **Cost:** mostly time. ~£0–£300/month if outsourcing on-page SEO tweaks. **Highest priority.**

### 6.2 Paid — **lean, accountable, switch on once tracking works**
- **Google Local Services Ads (LSA):** pay-per-lead, "Google Guaranteed" badge, sits above the Map Pack. Ideal for a trade — you pay for leads, not clicks. **Start here.** ~£300–£600/month.
- **Google Search Ads:** target high-intent commercial keywords (e.g. "property maintenance company London", "landlord maintenance SE London") that SEO won't rank for quickly. ~£300–£500/month, tightly geo-fenced to SE/South London.
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram) retargeting:** cheap brand reinforcement for homeowners/landlords who visited the site. Not for cold acquisition. ~£100–£200/month.
- **Total paid:** start ~£400/month, scale to ~£1,000+ once cost-per-lead and quote→win rates are proven.

### 6.3 Partnerships / B2B outreach — **highest leverage for recurring revenue**
This is where the company is built. Direct, human outreach to **letting agents, estate agents, block/property managers, and commercial facilities** in SE/South London.
- **Actions:** Build a target list of independent letting/managing agents within ~5 miles (Lewisham, Greenwich, Deptford, Blackheath, Catford, Bermondsey). Approach with a sharp one-pager: reliability, the job-tracking link they can forward, the per-property audit trail, planned-maintenance reporting. Offer a **no-risk trial job** to prove it. Follow up relentlessly but politely. Target a standing relationship, not a one-off.
- **Cost:** time + a printed/PDF one-pager (~£100 design, ~£50 print). Effectively free leads with the highest LTV in the business. **Co-priority #1 with local SEO.**

### 6.4 Referral programme & word-of-mouth
Trades live on referrals. Formalise it.
- **Actions:** "Recommend EGO, get £X off your next job" for homeowners; a thank-you fee or credit for agents/landlords who refer a peer. Ask for the referral *at the moment of the happy review*.
- **Cost:** funded from margin (~£20–£50 per converted referral). Near-zero fixed cost, high quality leads.

### 6.5 Hyperlocal physical — doorstep/leaflet + vehicle livery
- **Vehicle livery:** a clean, branded van is a moving billboard across SE London every day. **Do this early** — one-off ~£300–£800, then free forever.
- **Targeted leafleting:** around streets where EGO has just completed a visible job ("we've just done work on your street") — far higher response than blanket drops. ~£100–£300 per campaign.

### 6.6 Directories — Checkatrade / MyBuilder / Google
- List on **Checkatrade and/or MyBuilder** for homeowner trust and lead flow, but treat as **secondary** — they're competitive, can be pricey, and you don't own the customer. ~£70–£150/month. Prioritise pushing reviews to **Google** (which you own and which feeds the Map Pack) over the directories.

### 6.7 Content & email
EGO has a campaigns tool and a blog — use them to nurture, not to chase virality.
- **Blog/SEO content:** practical, search-friendly pieces — *winter property checklist, planned vs reactive maintenance (the landlord cost argument), how to choose a maintenance contractor, void turnaround checklist for landlords*. These rank, build authority, and arm the sales conversation.
- **Email:** segment by ICP. Landlords/agents get the planned-maintenance and compliance angle; homeowners get seasonal tips and care-plan offers. ~£0 (tool already exists) + writing time.

## 7. Lead-Gen Funnel & Conversion

**The funnel:**

1. **Discover** — found via Google search/Map Pack, LSA, referral, agent relationship, or van/leaflet.
2. **Enquire** — lands on a relevant service+area page → **"Get a quote"** (form + phone number visible everywhere).
3. **Quote in writing** — fast response, clear written fixed price. *Speed here is the #1 conversion lever for trades — first to respond often wins.*
4. **Win the job** — booked into an agreed window.
5. **Deliver** — job done properly, customer watching the **live tracking link**, tidy up.
6. **Invoice** — itemised, transparent, bank transfer.
7. **Review request** — automated at completion via the campaigns tool, one tap to Google.
8. **Retain / repeat** — care-plan offer, planned-maintenance pitch, referral ask.

**Conversion levers, ranked by impact:**

- **Response speed.** Aim to acknowledge every enquiry within **1 hour**, written quote within **24 hours**. Nothing converts trade leads better than being first and clear.
- **Written fixed price.** Removes the buyer's biggest fear and out-positions the "from £X" crowd.
- **The tracking link as a closing tool.** Show it in the quote — "here's what you'll see while we work." Tangible proof of the reliability promise.
- **Reviews on the landing pages.** Social proof at the decision point lifts form conversion.
- **A single, obvious call to action.** "Get a quote" + phone number on every page, above the fold, on mobile especially (most "near me" traffic is mobile).

## 8. Retention & LTV

One-off jobs are where competitors stop. EGO's whole technical edge is built to go further.

- **Turn homeowner one-offs into care plans.** After a successful job, offer a light **annual maintenance plan** (a yearly once-over, priority booking, small-job allowance). Converts a £150 call-out into a recurring relationship and a predictable revenue line.
- **Land planned-maintenance contracts** with landlords, agents, and commercial clients — the core LTV play. The app's **planned-maintenance scheduling + reporting** is the differentiator that justifies a contract: scheduled, documented, reported. Pitch it as *"prevent the emergency, and prove it was prevented."*
- **The flywheel:** job-tracking link → great experience → automated review request → public review → improved Map Pack ranking + referral → more leads → more jobs. **Every completed job should feed reviews, referrals, and a retention offer.** Bake these three asks into the job-completion workflow so they happen every time, not when someone remembers.
- **Per-property record as a switching cost.** Once an agent or landlord has a history with EGO across their portfolio, leaving means losing the audit trail and continuity. That record is a quiet but real moat — lean into it in the B2B pitch.
