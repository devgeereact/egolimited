How EGO LTD sounds, in writing and in person — and how to keep it that way. This is the single source of truth for any words that go out under our name: texts, emails, quotes, invoices, the website, reviews, the lot. If you're writing for EGO and you're not sure how it should read, this is the document you check.

## Voice mission statement

We sound like the reliable tradesperson who explains things clearly and always turns up. Plain, straight, no jargon, no salesy fluff. We're confident because we do the work properly — but we never brag about it. We lead with proof, not promises: the per-property record, the live tracking link, the itemised VAT invoice, the fixed price in writing. We respect the customer's time and their money, and our writing shows it. Quiet and premium, grounded, British. We say what we'll do, then we do it, then we show the receipt.

> **The one-line test:** would a careful landlord or a busy managing agent read this and think "good, these people are sorted" — without rolling their eyes once? If yes, it's on-brand.

## Voice attributes

Six things our writing always is. Each one has a plain definition, a do, a don't, and a real before/after.

### 1. Plain-spoken

**What it means:** We write the way a sensible person talks. Short words over long ones. The point first, not buried at the bottom. If a ten-year-old couldn't follow it, rewrite it.

- **Do:** Use everyday words. "We'll fix the leak" beats "we will undertake remedial works to address the water ingress."
- **Don't:** Reach for the formal or technical word to sound more professional. It does the opposite.

> **Before:** "Following our attendance, we have identified that remedial intervention is required in respect of the affected sanitary fixture."
> **After:** "We had a look — the toilet's cracked and needs replacing. Here's the cost."

### 2. Straight (honest and direct)

**What it means:** We tell people the actual situation, including the bits they won't love. No hedging, no weasel words, no hiding the price or the delay.

- **Do:** State the facts, the cost, the timing, and any catch — up front.
- **Don't:** Soften bad news into mush, or dress up a "we can't do that" as a maybe.

> **Before:** "We'll do our best to possibly get someone out at some point this week, costs may vary depending on a number of factors."
> **After:** "We can be there Thursday morning. It'll be £180 + VAT for the call-out and first hour. If it turns out to be a bigger job, we'll stop and tell you the new price before carrying on."

### 3. Proof-led

**What it means:** This is our edge. We don't ask people to trust us — we show them. Every claim points at something real: a record, a photo, a tracking link, a line on an invoice, a price in writing.

- **Do:** Back what you say with the receipt. "Here's the link to follow it" / "It's all itemised on the invoice" / "It's on the property's record."
- **Don't:** Use trust-me language — "best in the area", "you're in safe hands", "second to none." Empty without proof.

> **Before:** "You can trust EGO to always do a great job — we're the most reliable team around!"
> **After:** "You can follow the job live here, and every part and hour is itemised on the invoice. Nothing hidden."

### 4. Confident but modest

**What it means:** We know we're good and it shows in how calmly we write — no need to shout. No bravado, no exclamation marks doing the heavy lifting, no superlatives. Quiet competence.

- **Do:** State capability plainly. "We handle the jobs other firms won't book."
- **Don't:** Boast, hype, or pile on adjectives. One "we'll sort it" beats three "amazing, world-class, unbeatable."

> **Before:** "EGO is THE go-to, world-class maintenance team — nobody does it better!!!"
> **After:** "We do general maintenance across South-East London — the reactive repairs, the planned upkeep, and the small jobs others won't take. Properly."

### 5. Respectful of time and money

**What it means:** The reader is busy and paying. We get to the point, we never waste their attention, and we're transparent about cost. Every message earns its length.

- **Do:** Lead with what they need. Give the price clearly. Keep it short.
- **Don't:** Pad with pleasantries, bury the cost, or make them chase you for basic information.

> **Before:** "Hi there! Hope you're having a lovely day! We just wanted to reach out and touch base regarding the matter we discussed, and would love to circle back when convenient to explore next steps together!"
> **After:** "Morning — quick one. The quote's ready: £420 + VAT, fixed. Reply 'yes' and we'll book you in for next week."

### 6. British and grounded

**What it means:** We sound local, because we are — South-East London, homes and commercial. UK spelling, UK money, UK dates, UK understatement. No American gloss, no corporate gloss.

- **Do:** Write "organise", "£", "Tuesday 8 July", "no problem", "we'll get it sorted."
- **Don't:** Use "reach out", "touch base", "going forward", "awesome", or American spellings.

> **Before:** "Awesome! We'll go ahead and optimize your maintenance program and reach out Q3 to circle back."
> **After:** "Good. We'll get the planned upkeep organised and check in with you in the autumn."

## Tone shifts by context

The voice never changes. The tone — how warm, how brief, how formal — flexes a little with who we're talking to and why.

### By audience

**Homeowner.** Slightly warmer and more reassuring. They may not know the trade, so explain plainly and remove worry. Still no waffle.
> "No need to be in — our engineer will text when he's on the way, and you'll get the photos and invoice straight after."

**Agent / B2B (landlords, letting/managing agents, facilities).** Brisker, more efficient, proof and paperwork forward. They handle many properties and want certainty, an audit trail, and no surprises. Respect that they're a professional.
> "Job's logged against 14 Aldworth Road. Fixed price £390 + VAT, tracking link below, itemised invoice to follow on completion. Anything else on that property, just add it to the ticket."

### By message type

| Context | Tone | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote** | Clear, confident, no pressure | The price (fixed, in writing) and exactly what's included |
| **Invoice** | Precise, plain, transparent | The total, then the itemised breakdown + VAT |
| **Chasing payment** | Polite, firm, factual — never apologetic, never aggressive | The fact: invoice number, amount, due date, how to pay |
| **Marketing** | Quiet, grounded, proof-led | What we do and the proof — not adjectives |
| **Bad news / delay** | Honest, prompt, owning it | What's happened, the new plan, what we're doing about it |

**Chasing payment — get the balance right.** Firm is not rude. We state the facts and make paying easy. We don't grovel ("so sorry to bother you again!!") and we don't threaten.
> "Hi — invoice #1043 (£390 + VAT) was due on 20 June. Could you settle it this week? Pay by card or transfer here: [link]. Shout if there's a query on it."

**Bad news / delay — own it fast.** No excuses dressed as reasons. Say what went wrong, fix the plan, and where it's our fault, say so plainly.
> "Bad news on today — our van's broken down and we won't make your 2pm. That's on us. Next slot is tomorrow 8am, and I'll knock £30 off the call-out for the hassle. Does the morning work?"

## Vocabulary

### Words we use

Plain, grounded, proof words. Reach for these.

- **Action/honesty:** sorted, fixed, done properly, had a look, we'll handle it, no problem, on us, here's the cost, in writing, fixed price
- **Proof:** itemised, tracking link, on the record, the receipt, the breakdown, photos, you can follow it live, every part and hour
- **Service:** general maintenance, reactive repairs, planned upkeep, recurring/maintenance, the small jobs, call-out, on-site, completion
- **British connective tissue:** morning, cheers, no problem, get it sorted, shout if, quick one, just so you know

### Words we ban

If any of these appear in EGO copy, rewrite before it goes out.

- **Corporate jargon:** synergy, leverage, going forward, circle back, touch base, reach out, deep dive, bandwidth, ecosystem, solutions (as filler), holistic, streamline, optimise (as buzzword), best-in-class, value-add
- **Empty hype:** world-class, second to none, market-leading, premier, unbeatable, amazing, incredible, game-changing, revolutionary, cutting-edge, passionate about, the #1
- **Trust-me language (no proof):** trust us, you're in safe hands, rest assured, we promise, peace of mind (as a claim, not a feature)
- **Americanisms / spellings:** awesome, optimize, organize, color, center, gotten, "reach out", "this Fall"
- **Exclamation spam:** more than one "!" in a message. Usually zero. We're calm.

## Grammar and mechanics

- **Spelling:** British English always. organise, colour, licence (noun), apologise, programme, neighbourhood, kerb.
- **Money:** Always `£` symbol, no space: `£180`. State VAT clearly — `£180 + VAT` or `£216 inc. VAT`. Never hide it.
- **Dates:** Day-month-year, written out where there's room: `Tuesday 8 July`, or `08/07/2026` in tables. Never US `07/08`.
- **Times:** 12-hour with am/pm, no full stops: `8am`, `2:30pm`, `morning`/`afternoon` for windows.
- **Phone:** `07539 705576`. Email: `hello@egolimited.com`.
- **Sentence length:** Short. One idea per sentence. If a sentence runs past about 20 words, split it. Mix short and very short for rhythm.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences. White space is kind to a busy reader.
- **Punctuation:** Full stops, not exclamation marks. Em dashes and commas for asides are fine. No ALL CAPS for emphasis — use plain words.
- **Numbers:** Spell out one to nine in prose; use figures for prices, measurements, dates, and anything technical.
- **Contractions:** Yes — we'll, you're, it's, don't. They keep us human. (Not in legal/invoice line items, where we stay precise.)
- **Oxford comma:** Use it when it aids clarity, drop it when it doesn't. Clarity wins.

## Messaging pillars and approved one-liners

Three pillars carry everything we say. Under each, lines you can lift straight into copy.

### Pillar 1 — Reactive repairs (when something breaks)
*The fast, reliable fix when something's gone wrong.*

- Homeowner: "Something broken? We'll have a look, give you a fixed price, and sort it — no surprises on the bill."
- Agent/B2B: "Reactive repairs across your portfolio, logged per property, fixed price in writing, tracked end to end."

### Pillar 2 — Planned & recurring upkeep (keeping it right)
*The maintenance that stops small problems becoming big ones.*

- Homeowner: "We'll keep on top of the upkeep so you don't have to think about it."
- Agent/B2B: "Planned, recurring maintenance with a clean record per property — fewer emergencies, a full audit trail, predictable cost."

### Pillar 3 — The small jobs others won't book
*The wedge. The half-day jobs other firms turn down.*

- Homeowner: "The small jobs other firms won't take? That's exactly what we book in."
- Agent/B2B: "The odd small job that's hard to get covered — we'll take it, log it, and invoice it like any other."

### The brand line
> **The jobs that need doing, done properly.**

Use it as a sign-off, a strapline, or a full stop on a pitch. Don't water it down or pad it.

### The proof wedge (use across all three pillars)
> "Per-property record, live job tracking, itemised VAT invoices, fixed price in writing. Here's the receipt — not 'trust me'."

## Example library

Good vs bad, for the formats we write most.

### 1. SMS-style job update

**Bad:**
> "Hi!!! Just to let you know our AMAZING engineer is going to reach out shortly to touch base about your job!! Have a great day!! 😊"

**Good:**
> "Morning — Tom's on his way to you, should be with you by 9:30. He'll text when he's outside."

### 2. Email (booking confirmation, homeowner)

**Bad:**
> "Dear Valued Customer, We are thrilled and excited to confirm we will be leveraging our world-class team to deliver a best-in-class solution for your property going forward! We are passionate about exceeding your expectations!!!"

**Good:**
> Subject: Booked in — Thursday 8am
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> You're booked for Thursday 8 July, 8am. Tom will be doing the work and will text when he's on his way — no need to be in if you'd rather not.
>
> Fixed price is £180 + VAT, as quoted. If we find anything bigger once we're on-site, we'll stop and tell you the new price before going ahead.
>
> You'll get photos and an itemised invoice as soon as it's done.
>
> Cheers,
> EGO LTD · 07539 705576

### 3. Quote intro (agent / B2B)

**Bad:**
> "Thank you for the opportunity! We'd love to partner with you on this exciting project and are confident we can deliver unbeatable value and synergy across your portfolio!"

**Good:**
> "Thanks for the details on 14 Aldworth Road. Here's a fixed quote — £390 + VAT, broken down below so you can see exactly what's included. The job's already logged against the property, so once you approve it you'll get a live tracking link and an itemised invoice on completion. No surprises on the final bill."

### 4. Review request

**Bad:**
> "We'd be SO grateful if you could leave us a 5-star review!!! It would mean the world and help our amazing small business grow!! 🙏🙏🙏"

**Good:**
> "Hope the repair's holding up well. If you've a minute, a quick honest review helps other people round here find us: [link]. No worries if you're busy — and shout if anything's not right."

### 5. Apology for a delay

**Bad:**
> "We sincerely apologise for any inconvenience caused. Due to circumstances beyond our control, your appointment has been impacted. We appreciate your patience and understanding during this time."

**Good:**
> "Quick heads-up — we're running behind today and won't make your 2pm slot. That's on us, sorry. We can come first thing tomorrow, 8am, or push to Friday afternoon if that's easier. Which suits? I'll knock £30 off the call-out for mucking you about."

## ENFORCEMENT

How we keep every word on-brand. Nothing goes out under EGO's name without passing this.

### Pre-publish brand-voice checklist

Run through this before sending. All ten should be yes.

- [ ] **Point first?** The reader gets what they need in the first line or two.
- [ ] **Plain words?** No jargon, no formal-for-formal's-sake, no technical fog.
- [ ] **Proof, not promises?** Any claim is backed by a record, link, invoice, photo, or price in writing.
- [ ] **Price clear?** If money's involved, the cost and VAT are stated plainly — not hidden, not vague.
- [ ] **No bravado?** No superlatives, no hype, no "world-class", no trust-me language.
- [ ] **Calm punctuation?** Zero or one exclamation mark. No ALL CAPS. No emoji spam.
- [ ] **British?** UK spelling, £, UK dates, no "reach out / touch base / awesome".
- [ ] **Short?** Sentences tight, paragraphs 1–3 lines, nothing padded.
- [ ] **Honest?** Bad news (if any) is stated straight and owned, not softened to mush.
- [ ] **Right tone for the reader?** Warmer for homeowners, brisker + paperwork-forward for agents.

### Scoring rubric (0–5 per attribute)

Score the copy on each of the six attributes. **0** = breaks it outright, **5** = textbook EGO.

| Attribute | 0–1 (fail) | 2–3 (weak) | 4–5 (on-brand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain-spoken** | Jargon-heavy, hard to follow | Some clunky/formal phrasing | Clear, everyday, point-first |
| **Straight** | Evasive, hides cost or catch | Hedges a bit | Honest, direct, nothing buried |
| **Proof-led** | "Trust us" with no evidence | Claims with thin backing | Every claim has a receipt |
| **Confident but modest** | Boastful, hype, superlatives | A stray adjective or two | Calm, capable, no bragging |
| **Respectful of time/money** | Padded, vague on price | Slightly long or soft on cost | Tight, price crystal clear |
| **British & grounded** | Americanisms, corporate gloss | Minor slips | Local, plain, UK throughout |

**Pass mark:** 24/30 overall, **and** no single attribute below 3, **and** zero red-flag words (below). Anything under that gets rewritten, not "nearly sent."

### Red-flag words — auto-fail

If any of these appear, the copy fails on the spot and must be rewritten before it goes out. No exceptions.

- synergy · leverage · world-class · best-in-class · second to none · market-leading · unbeatable · game-changing · cutting-edge · revolutionary
- reach out · touch base · circle back · going forward · deep dive · bandwidth · holistic · streamline
- awesome · amazing (as filler) · incredible · passionate about · rest assured · you're in safe hands · trust us
- any American spelling (optimize, organize, color, center) · two or more "!" in one message · ALL-CAPS words for emphasis · emoji spam (more than one, or any in B2B)

### How to review any copy against this — the 4-step process

1. **Read it cold, as the customer.** Forget you wrote it. A busy landlord opens this on their phone — do they get what they need in five seconds, and does it make them trust us more? Note your gut reaction.
2. **Scan for red flags.** Ctrl-F the banned list above. One hit = stop, rewrite that bit, restart the scan. This is fast and catches most problems.
3. **Score the six attributes.** Use the rubric. Be honest — 3 is "fine", not "good". Add up. Below 24, or any attribute under 3? It's not ready.
4. **Run the checklist, then cut.** Tick off all ten boxes. Then read once more and delete anything that isn't pulling its weight — a whole sentence, ideally. Shorter is almost always more EGO.

> **When in doubt, ask:** "Am I telling them, or selling them?" We tell. We show the receipt. We get it sorted. Then we're done.
