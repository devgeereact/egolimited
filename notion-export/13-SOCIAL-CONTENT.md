A practical social plan for EGO LTD — a small South-East London maintenance firm. The goal isn't to go viral; it's to look reliable, win recurring B2B work (landlords, letting/managing agents, facilities), and pick up local homeowners. Keep it quiet, premium and proof-led — show the work, the fixed price in writing, the live tracking link, the tidy invoice. Post less, post better.

## Platforms & Why

| Platform | Who it reaches | Why it earns its place | Priority |
|----------|----------------|------------------------|----------|
| **Instagram** | Local homeowners, design-minded folk, neighbours | Before/after work is naturally visual; Stories keep us present without much effort | High |
| **Facebook** | Homeowners 35+, local community, FB local groups | Biggest local-area reach in SE London; reviews live here; groups = warm leads | High |
| **LinkedIn** | Letting/managing agents, landlords, commercial/FM, property managers | This is where the recurring B2B prize is. Reliability, compliance, paperwork, SLAs land here | High (B2B) |
| **Google Business Profile** | People actively searching "handyman / maintenance near me" | Highest-intent of all. Posts + photos + reviews directly feed local search ranking | High |
| **Nextdoor** | Hyperlocal homeowners, recommendations | Neighbours ask "who do you use for…" — being present wins referrals | Medium |

Not doing: TikTok / chasing viral. Wrong audience, wrong tone, wrong time cost for a small firm. Revisit only if we ever hire someone who genuinely enjoys making short video.

**One golden rule:** every job is a content opportunity. The engineer's phone on site is the camera. No on-site photos = no posts. Everything downstream depends on that habit.

Realistic for a small firm: batch-write on a Monday, schedule the week. ~20–30 mins/day max. Don't burn out — consistency beats volume.

## Content Pillars

1. **Before / After jobs** — the bread and butter. Real work, real homes/units, clean transformation. Carpentry, decorating, tiling, repairs.
2. **How we work / fixed-price proof** — the wedge made visible: quote in writing, no surprise costs, itemised VAT invoice, per-property record. This is what separates us from a bloke with a van.
3. **The app & live tracking link** — "know exactly when we're coming and what we did." Tracking link, photo log, digital invoice. Modern, transparent, premium.
4. **Customer reviews / proof** — turn every 5-star into a post. Words from real customers do the selling for us.
5. **Local & community** — South-East London pride. Areas we cover, local landmarks, "spotted us in…", supporting the patch.
6. **Seasonal maintenance tips** — genuinely useful upkeep advice by season. Builds authority, gets saved/shared, soft sells planned upkeep.
7. **B2B / landlord & agent focus (LinkedIn-led)** — void turnarounds, compliance-minded repairs, planned maintenance contracts, one point of contact, paperwork that survives an audit.

## Before / After Post Template

Reusable structure — fill the brackets, keep it short and concrete.

> **[Honest description of the "before" problem]** → **[the result].**
> [One line on how we did it — the bit that shows care: dust sheets, took the rubbish, did it in a day, priced up front.]
> [Optional: the EGO wedge — quote in writing / tracking link / itemised invoice.]
> [CTA: Call 07539 705576 · hello@egolimited.com · egolimited.com]
> [3–6 SE London + trade hashtags]

**Image rules:**
- Same angle, same framing for before and after. Shoot the "before" from where you'll shoot the "after."
- Good light — open curtains, lights on, no shadow of you in the shot.
- Tidy the frame before the "after" (no tools, no dust, no clutter).
- Carousel order: before first, after second. Or a clean side-by-side.
- Never show anything identifying the property/tenant without permission (door numbers, post, personal items).

## Review-to-Social Workflow

Turn every happy customer into proof. Make it a habit, not a one-off.

1. **Ask at completion.** When a job's signed off and the customer's pleased, the engineer/office asks for a Google review — send the direct review link by email same day.
2. **Catch it.** New 5-star review lands (Google, Facebook, Checkatrade-type, or a nice email/text).
3. **Screenshot / pull the quote.** Grab the strongest line. Anonymise to first name + area if needed ("Sarah, Greenwich").
4. **Drop into the review template graphic.** Stone & Ember brand: near-black background, scarce ember-orange accent, the quote in clean type, small EGO mark. Keep one reusable template.
5. **Pair with a job photo** where possible — the actual work the review is about. Proof + proof.
6. **Post.** Instagram + Facebook feed, repost to Stories, post to Google Business Profile (reviews feed local ranking directly), and the strong B2B ones to LinkedIn.
7. **Reply to the review publicly** too — a warm, human thank-you. That's content as well, and it shows you care.
8. **Log it.** Keep a simple folder/sheet of approved review quotes so you're never short of post material.

> Aim: never let a genuinely great review pass without it becoming at least one post.

## Engineer On-Site Shot List

Print this. Stick it in the van. The phone in your pocket is the marketing department.

**Every job, no exceptions:**
- [ ] **Before** — wide shot of the problem area, from a fixed spot you can return to.
- [ ] **Before** — one close-up of the actual issue (the crack, the leak, the worn bit).
- [ ] **After** — same wide shot, same angle, frame tidied.
- [ ] **After** — same close-up, showing it sorted.

**Bonus when you can (no rush, no fuss):**
- [ ] A 10–15 sec clip of a satisfying step (peeling tape off a clean paint line, the first tile going on, the door finally clicking shut).
- [ ] A neat "tools down, job done" shot of the finished area.
- [ ] The tidy-up — taking rubbish away, sheets coming off. People love this; it signals respect for the home.
- [ ] The van / team on site (with consent) for local + brand posts.

**Rules of the road:**
- Landscape for feed, vertical for Stories/Reels. Grab both if quick.
- Wipe the lens. Steady hands. Good light beats a fancy phone.
- **Ask permission** before photographing inside someone's home/unit, every time.
- **No identifying details** in shot — door numbers, post, kids' photos, personal clutter. When in doubt, reframe.
- Send to the shared album / office same day while it's fresh. No photos = no post.
