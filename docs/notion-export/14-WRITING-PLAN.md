This is the editorial and SEO content roadmap for EGO LTD — what we write, who it is for, when it ships, and how we know it worked. It builds on the SEO audit (02) and optimiser (03) and the marketing strategy (05); it does not repeat them. Those documents cover the technical SEO fixes (prerendering the SPA, sitemap, JSON-LD) and the channel/budget plan. This one is about the words: the articles, guides, service and area copy, and the lead magnets that earn trust and rankings for a South-East London maintenance firm whose primary prize is recurring B2B work.

## 1. Content goals

Three goals, in priority order. Every piece we write must move at least one.

1. **Win recurring B2B trust.** The money is in property care plans and commercial planned maintenance — landlords, letting and managing agents, facilities managers. These buyers do not impulse-hire off a blog post; they read to reassure themselves a supplier is competent, insured, and won't embarrass them in front of a tenant or a director. Content's job here is **credibility and de-risking**, not clicks.
2. **Rank for local intent.** Capture South-East London people searching for a trade plus an area ("carpenter Lewisham", "leak repair Greenwich", "end of tenancy repairs SE10"). The service and area pages do the heavy lifting; informational guides feed them internal links and topical authority so they rank.
3. **Convert the homeowner who is ready now.** A homeowner searching "how much does it cost to fix a leaking tap" or "do I need to reseal my bath" is one short article and one clear CTA away from a quote. These are quick wins that fund the slower B2B play.

Guard-rails (from the brand pack — non-negotiable): plain British English, give a price and a date, no jargon, no em dashes, and never publish a claim the business cannot back up. No invented case studies, no fake review counts, no "we've completed 5,000 jobs" until it is true. Honesty is the differentiator; the content must sound like it.

## 2. Audience → topic mapping

Four audiences, each with a different worry and a different question they type into Google.

| Audience | What keeps them up at night | Questions they search | What we write for them | Funnel weight |

| **Landlord** (1–10 units) | Void days, tenant complaints, compliance | "end of tenancy repairs", "landlord maintenance checklist", "who fixes small jobs between tenants" | Checklists, void-turnaround guides, planned vs reactive cost pieces | MOFU/BOFU, high value |

The homeowner content is the volume play. The agent and FM content is the margin play. We deliberately over-index on landlord/agent material relative to its search volume, because one signed property care plan is worth dozens of one-off homeowner jobs.

## 3. Content pillars & funnel stages

Four pillars map onto the three business lines (reactive repairs, planned/recurring upkeep, small jobs) plus a trust pillar that underpins the B2B sell.

- **Pillar A — Reactive repairs & costs.** "What does it cost, how fast, do I need a specialist." Mostly homeowner, mostly TOFU/BOFU. Feeds the service pages.
- **Pillar B — Planned & recurring upkeep.** "Why scheduled maintenance is cheaper, what a plan covers, seasonal checklists." Landlord/agent/FM, MOFU. This is the pillar that sells care plans.
- **Pillar C — Small jobs & the awkward list.** "The half-day jobs nobody books in." Homeowner and landlord, TOFU. Low competition, easy to own, very on-brand.
- **Pillar D — Trust, pricing & how we work.** "Fixed prices in writing, insured, per-property records, the tracking link." Cuts across every audience, mostly BOFU. This is where the wedge (per-property record + live job-tracking link + itemised VAT invoices + fixed price in writing) gets explained.

Funnel stages and what "good" looks like:

- **TOFU (awareness)** — informational guides, checklists, "how much / how to / when to". Goal: rank, get found, internal-link to services. Measured by organic sessions + assisted conversions.
- **MOFU (consideration)** — comparison and process pieces, downloadable guides, lead magnets gated behind a one-field email. Goal: capture a landlord/agent email, prove competence. Measured by lead-magnet downloads + email signups.
- **BOFU (decision)** — service×area pages, pricing explainers, "what to expect", FAQ. Goal: quote request. Measured by quote-form submissions + calls.

Every article carries one primary CTA matched to its stage: TOFU → "get a free quote" or "download the checklist"; MOFU → "book a property care call"; BOFU → "get a price and a date".

## 6. Briefs for the three highest-priority articles

These three are first because each one carries the most weight against the primary B2B prize plus one fast homeowner win.

### Brief 1 — The real cost of an end-of-tenancy repair list in South-East London

- **Target keyword:** end of tenancy repairs cost London (secondary: void property repairs, snagging list cost)
- **Search intent:** Commercial-investigational. A landlord or agent between tenancies wants to know what a repair list typically costs, how long it takes, and whether one contractor can handle the lot. They are price-anchoring and supplier-shopping at the same time.
- **Word count:** 1,100–1,400
- **Outline (H2s):**
  - H2: What ends up on a typical end-of-tenancy list (the recurring offenders — sticking doors, failed sealant, scuffed walls, loose fittings)
  - H2: Why empty days cost more than the repairs do (frame the real cost as void days, not the invoice)
  - H2: One price for the whole list vs paying five trades (the EGO model: per-property, one visit count, one itemised invoice)
  - H2: A realistic example list and what drives the price (honest ranges, "price on request", no invented figures)
  - H2: How to keep a record your landlord or compliance file will accept (per-property record + photos + the tracking link)
  - H2: Getting a fixed price before anyone starts
- **Internal links:** Property Turnarounds service page; Planned Maintenance service page; "Planned or reactive: what actually costs less" (existing); a relevant area page (e.g. void repairs in Greenwich); the quote/contact page.
- **CTA:** "Send us the list and book a property care call — one price, one visit count, a clear record per property."

### Brief 2 — The landlord's planned-maintenance checklist (lead magnet + companion post)

- **Target keyword:** landlord maintenance checklist (secondary: rental property maintenance schedule, landlord planned maintenance)
- **Search intent:** Informational with a download intent. A landlord wants a practical, season-by-season list they can actually use — and is willing to give an email for a clean PDF version.
- **Word count:** 900–1,200 for the companion post; the PDF is the asset.
- **Outline (H2s):**
  - H2: Why a checklist beats waiting for the phone to ring (planned is cheaper than reactive — link the existing comparison piece)
  - H2: Monthly and quarterly checks that take minutes
  - H2: The seasonal jobs that prevent the expensive ones (gutters, seals, heating before winter)
  - H2: Compliance-adjacent items to keep on your radar (safety-first framing; defer gas/electrical to certified specialists, per brand)
  - H2: What to hand to a contractor — and what to keep for your records
  - H2: Download the full checklist (one-field email gate)
- **Internal links:** Property Care Plan / Planned Maintenance pages; "A winter checklist for landlords" (existing); Minor Plumbing and Exterior & Grounds service pages; pricing/packages.
- **CTA:** "Download the checklist" → email capture → automated follow-up offering a property care call.

### Brief 3 — What does it cost to fix a leaking tap? (London, 2026)

- **Target keyword:** cost to fix a leaking tap (secondary: dripping tap repair cost London, plumber to fix tap)
- **Search intent:** Transactional-informational. A homeowner has a dripping tap, wants a ballpark, and is close to booking. High intent, fast win, feeds the Minor Plumbing service page.
- **Word count:** 700–900 (concise on purpose — they want the answer)
- **Outline (H2s):**
  - H2: What actually causes a dripping tap (washer, cartridge, seal — quick plain-English)
  - H2: What it typically costs to put right (honest framing: most are a quick, non-gas fix; "price on request"/clear estimate, no fabricated numbers)
  - H2: When it's a five-minute job and when it's a sign of something bigger
  - H2: Why a small leak is worth fixing now (wasted water, damage behind tiles, link to resealing)
  - H2: How EGO prices and books it (fixed price in writing, most jobs same week)
- **Internal links:** Minor Plumbing service page; Walls & Tiling (resealing); "what to reseal a bath" article (M3, once live); a local area page; quote/contact.
- **CTA:** "Get a price and a date — most repairs booked the same week."

## 7. Lead-magnet & guide ideas

Gated assets exist to turn anonymous B2B readers into emails we can nurture. Keep the gate light — one field, business email — and the asset genuinely useful, not a sales sheet.

- **The landlord's planned-maintenance checklist** (PDF) — season-by-season, printable. The flagship magnet (see Brief 2). Primary capture for the landlord audience.
- **The letting agent's maintenance pack** — a one-pager on how EGO reports per property, the tracking link, response windows, and the single-contact model. Aimed at agents evaluating a supplier; doubles as sales-enablement collateral.
- **Void-turnaround timeline template** — an editable checklist agents/landlords can use to run a turnaround from key handback to ready-to-let. High utility, very on-brand.
- **Commercial planned-maintenance ROI one-pager** — planned vs reactive cost framing for an FM or office manager building a business case internally. Helps them sell EGO inside their own organisation.
- **"How to read a maintenance quote" explainer** — short PDF demystifying fixed price vs day rate vs materials and what an itemised VAT invoice should show. Trust asset; works for every audience and reinforces the pricing wedge.

Delivery: each magnet sits behind a simple form, fires an email with the asset, and tags the contact by audience so the follow-up sequence (handled in the campaigns/email system already in the app) is relevant. Track downloads as the MOFU conversion.

## 8. Writing process & brief template

A repeatable loop so quality stays consistent whether the owner writes it or it's drafted with AI and edited.

**The loop (per piece):**
1. **Pick from the calendar** — confirm the target keyword still has intent and isn't already covered.
2. **Fill the brief template** (below) — no writing starts without a brief.
3. **Draft** — plain British English, the brand voice, real specifics over filler. AI draft is fine as a starting point; it is never the finished article.
4. **Edit against the guard-rails** — give a price and a date, cut jargon, remove any claim the business can't back up, strip em dashes, check it sounds like a person who does the work.
5. **SEO pass** — title ≤60 chars (brand included), meta 150–160 chars, one H1, sensible H2s, target keyword in title + first 100 words + one H2, internal links placed, alt text on images. Align tags with the `serviceSeo`/`articleSeo` pattern already in `content.ts`.
6. **Add to CMS / content hub** (`blog.php`) or the bundled `articles` fallback, set slug, excerpt, cover image.
7. **Publish, then internal-link** — add links from related existing pages pointing to the new piece (don't leave it orphaned).
8. **Log it** — record publish date, keyword, and CTA in the calendar so measurement is possible.

## 9. Cadence & ownership

- **Cadence:** 2 published pieces / month (1 B2B, 1 homeowner) + ongoing area-page depth + 1 refresh. Roughly 4–6 focused hours a week. Hold the line at two solid pieces over four thin ones.
- **Owner (accountable):** Gideon / EGO — final voice, sign-off on every claim, and the only person who can approve a new pricing figure going public.
- **Drafting:** AI-assisted first drafts against the brief, always human-edited to the brand voice before publish. Never publish an unedited draft.
- **SEO alignment:** every piece checked against the optimiser doc (03) conventions — titles/meta, JSON-LD, internal linking — so content and technical SEO pull together.
- **Review rhythm:** monthly — what published, what ranked, what converted; reprioritise the next month from the data, not the plan.

## 10. How we measure

Tie every piece back to the three goals. Vanity metrics (raw pageviews) are secondary to leads.

- **Rankings (TOFU/BOFU):** track positions for the service×area terms and the cluster head terms in Search Console. Win = climbing into the top 10, then top 3, for "{service} {area}" queries.
- **Organic traffic & engagement:** organic sessions to blog + service/area pages, and whether people read (scroll depth / time) or bounce. Thin area pages will show in the bounce numbers — that's the signal to deepen them.
- **Lead-magnet conversions (MOFU):** downloads per magnet, and email signups by audience tag. This is the primary B2B metric — an email from a landlord or agent is worth more than 100 homeowner pageviews.
- **Quote requests & calls (BOFU):** form submissions and calls attributed to a content page (first-touch and assisted). The number that actually matters.
- **Assisted conversions:** which articles appear in the path to a booked job, even if they weren't the last click — credits the TOFU pieces that do the quiet work.
- **Per-piece scorecard, reviewed monthly:** for each published article — keyword, current rank, organic sessions, downloads, quote requests assisted. Kill or rewrite anything that's done nothing after 90 days; double down on whatever's pulling leads.

North-star for content over the first six months: a steady flow of **B2B email captures** (landlords + agents) from the lead magnets, and at least a handful of the service×area pages ranking page-one for their local term. Everything else is in service of those two.
