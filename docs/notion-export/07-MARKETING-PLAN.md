This is the execution playbook — the *how* behind the strategy in `05-MARKETING-STRATEGY.md`. Channels, the 90-day launch, a 12-month calendar, where the money goes, the weekly rhythm, and the numbers we hold ourselves to. Built for one operator (plus a part-time hand) on a real small-business budget.

## How to use this document

The strategy says *win recurring B2B in SE London, fund it with homeowner proof-and-cashflow work, lean on the "here's the receipt" wedge*. This plan turns that into things you do on a Tuesday morning. Everything below assumes one person running marketing alongside the day job, so it's deliberately ruthless about what earns its place. If a tactic doesn't either (a) generate a lead this month or (b) compound the reputation moat, it's not here.

Two numbers to keep in your head all year: **40–60 qualified leads/month by month 12**, and **6–10 recurring B2B accounts signed**. Every channel below is judged against those.

## 1. Channel-by-channel plan

Channels are ranked by what actually moves the needle for a local trade in SE London. Spend and effort follow that ranking — not the other way round.

### 1.1 Google Business Profile + local SEO — the single highest-ROI channel

For a local maintenance firm, the Google Map Pack is the shop window. Most homeowner and small-landlord searches ("handyman Lewisham", "painter decorator SE13", "carpenter near me") get answered from the three-result map pack before anyone scrolls to the website results. Win that and leads arrive for free. This is priority one, and it's nearly all sweat, not spend.

**Setup (week 1, do it properly once):**
- Claim and fully complete the Google Business Profile for EGO LTD at 11 Halesworth Road, London SE13 7TJ. Verify by postcard/phone.
- Primary category: **Handyman**. Secondary categories: Carpenter, Painter, Plumber, Tiling contractor, Property maintenance. Add every one that's truthfully relevant — categories are a ranking factor.
- Service area: list the SE London boroughs and postcode districts you cover — Lewisham, Greenwich, Southwark, Bromley, and the SE postcodes (SE1–SE28). Don't claim all of London; relevance beats reach.
- Services: add each trade as a named service with a 2–3 line description and an indicative "from" price where honest. Carpentry & joinery, painting & decorating, minor plumbing, walls & tiling, plus "small / half-day jobs".
- Hours: Mon–Fri 8am–6pm. Add holiday hours when they change.
- Photos: 15–20 to start. Real jobs — before/after, the van, the team in branded clothing, a tidied-up finish. Geotag where you can. This is the cheapest trust signal you have.
- Booking/contact: phone `07539 705576`, `hello@egolimited.com`, website, and the messaging button on.

**Ongoing (the bit most firms skip):**
- **Reviews are the ranking lever.** Target 50+ genuine Google reviews in 12 months — roughly 4–5/month. Ask *every* satisfied customer, at the moment the job's signed off, with a one-tap link (QR on the invoice/completion email). Reply to every review within 48 hours, name the job, stay human. Never buy or fake them.
- Post a **Google Post weekly** — a recent job, a seasonal tip, a service spotlight. Keeps the profile "active", which helps.
- Add a fresh batch of job photos every fortnight.
- Keep NAP (name, address, phone) *identical* everywhere online — website footer, directories, social. Inconsistency quietly kills local ranking.

**On-site local SEO (the website does its share):**
- One dedicated, properly-written page per trade × priority area — e.g. `/carpentry-joinery-lewisham`, `/painting-decorating-greenwich`. Real copy, real photos, FAQs, a clear quote CTA. Don't spin thin doorway pages; write them like you'd explain the job to a customer.
- Embed the Google map, NAP, opening hours, and LocalBusiness schema on every page.
- A short, honest "areas we cover" page listing the boroughs/postcodes.
- Page titles in the pattern `[Trade] in [Area] — Fixed Prices, Fully Insured | EGO LTD`.

### 1.2 Google Ads (Local Services + Search) — buy the high-intent searches you can't yet rank for

Organic ranking takes months. Ads buy you the top of the page on day one for people actively looking — which is exactly the homeowner/landlord searching "emergency joiner SE13" right now. Start small, prove a cost-per-lead, then scale.

- **Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — do this first.** Pay-per-lead, sits *above* the map pack, carries the "Google Guaranteed" badge (passing the background/insurance check is worth it for trust alone). Ideal for trades. Budget tightly to start; you only pay for genuine lead contacts and can dispute junk ones.
- **Search Ads — tight and local.** Don't run broad. Exact/phrase match on high-intent commercial terms: `[carpenter lewisham]`, `[handyman se london]`, `"painter decorator greenwich"`, `[property maintenance landlord london]`. Geo-target the boroughs you serve, radius-tight around SE13.
- **Negative keywords from day one:** `free`, `jobs`, `salary`, `course`, `diy`, `materials only`, `wholesale`, `parts` — strip out the tyre-kickers and job-seekers.
- **Landing pages, not the homepage.** Each ad group points at its matching trade-area page with one job: get the call or the quote form. Click-to-call prominent for mobile.
- **Conversion tracking on before you spend a penny** — calls and form fills both. Flying blind on ad spend is how a £400 budget evaporates.
- Start at the low end of the budget (~£200–£300/mo across LSA + Search), watch cost-per-lead, kill what doesn't convert, double down on what does.

### 1.3 Organic content / SEO — slow-burn authority that pays for years

Content is the long game that makes every other channel cheaper over time. The goal isn't blogging for its own sake — it's owning the answers to the questions your customers and your B2B buyers actually type.

- **Homeowner-intent guides** (capture search + show competence): "How much does it cost to fix a sticking door in London?", "Do I need a certified electrician for X?", "Repaint or touch up — what's worth it in a rental?". Honest, specific, priced where possible.
- **B2B-intent pieces** (the ones that win agents): "A landlord's reactive-repair checklist for void turnarounds", "What a planned maintenance schedule should cover for a small commercial site", "What good maintenance record-keeping looks like (and why it protects you at check-out)". These double as sales collateral.
- **The wedge content** — show the receipt. A page/post walking through exactly what a customer gets: the written fixed price, the live tracking link, the itemised VAT invoice, the per-property record. Screenshots of the app (anonymised). This *is* the differentiator; make it visible.
- Cadence: **2 solid pieces/month**. Quality over volume — two genuinely useful pages beat ten thin ones.
- Repurpose every piece: a Google Post, a couple of social posts, a line in the monthly email. Write once, use five times.

### 1.4 Direct B2B outreach to agents & landlords — the real growth engine

This is where the recurring revenue actually comes from, and it's the channel most local trades neglect because it's harder than waiting for the phone to ring. It's also the highest-leverage thing in this entire plan: one managing agent can be worth fifty call-outs.

**Build the list (week 2 onward):**
- Map every **independent letting/managing agent** within ~5 miles of SE13 — Lewisham, Blackheath, Greenwich, New Cross, Catford, Forest Hill, Brockley, Deptford. Walk the high streets, note the names, pull their details. Aim for an initial list of **50–80 agents**.
- Add **block managers / managing agents** for local residential blocks, and **facilities/office managers** for small commercial sites (cafés, salons, clinics, small offices, community buildings).
- Add **portfolio landlords** you can identify (local landlord forums, BTL groups, word of mouth).
- Keep it all in the CRM with owner name, role, last contact, status.

**The outreach motion (this is a *sequence*, not a one-off email):**
1. **Warm physical drop.** Print a clean one-pager — the "here's the receipt" proof, the trades covered, insurance, fixed-price-in-writing, the per-property record, contact. Hand-deliver to local agent offices. In a trust business, a face and a tidy leave-behind beats a cold email ten to one.
2. **Follow-up email** 2–3 days later, referencing the drop-in. Short. One ask: a 15-minute chat or a trial job. Link to the proof page.
3. **Trial-job offer** — the wedge that opens the door: *"Give us one void/repair to handle. You'll get a fixed price in writing, a live tracking link, and an itemised invoice with a full property record. No tie-in. If you're not impressed, you've lost nothing."* This de-risks them entirely and lets the app sell itself.
4. **Phone follow-up** a week later. Then add to a light monthly nurture (the email below).
5. **Convert trial → standing arrangement.** Once they've used you twice and seen the record-keeping, propose a simple preferred-supplier or planned-maintenance arrangement.

- Target volume: **8–12 new agent/landlord contacts entered into the sequence per week** once running. Track meetings booked and trials won, not emails sent.
- A light **monthly B2B email** to the whole list: a quick proof point (recent void turned around in X days), a seasonal nudge (gutters before winter, void-prep in summer), one clear CTA. Built in the app's bulk-email tool.

### 1.5 Referral — the cheapest leads you'll ever get

Trade work runs on word of mouth. Make it deliberate instead of accidental.

- **Customer referral:** at job sign-off, ask, and make it easy — "know anyone who needs something doing? Send them our way." A small thank-you (e.g. £20 off their next job, or a genuine thank-you note) for a referral that converts. Keep it simple; don't over-engineer a "scheme".
- **Agent referral:** a happy agent tells other agents. Ask satisfied B2B clients directly whether they know a counterpart who's frustrated with their current lot.
- **Trade cross-referral:** build relationships with trades you *don't* do or won't take on (gas engineers, electricians for notifiable work, roofers, locksmiths). You refer to them, they refer the general-maintenance stuff to you. Reciprocal, and it plugs the gaps in your offer.
- Track where every lead came from so you know which referrers to nurture.

### 1.6 Directories & Checkatrade-type platforms — proof and discovery, chosen carefully

These are trust-and-discovery channels, not the core engine. Be selective — some are expensive for what they return. Pick two or three, do them well, don't spread thin.

- **Checkatrade** — the recognised UK name; carries weight with homeowners and some landlords. Worth the membership *if* you actively gather reviews there. Test for a year, judge on leads.
- **Bark / MyBuilder / Rated People** — pay-per-lead platforms. Useful for early cashflow and reviews while organic ranks. Watch cost-per-lead hard; quality is variable. Treat as a tap you turn down once GBP + referrals fill the pipeline.
- **Free, do-anyway citations:** Yell, Thomson Local, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Cylex, FreeIndex, and any Lewisham/Greenwich local business directories. These mostly exist for the consistent-NAP citation benefit to local SEO — set them up once, keep them identical, move on.
- **Trade-credibility listings:** if/when you join relevant bodies (e.g. TrustMark, a trade association), list there — it's a trust signal for cautious B2B buyers.

### 1.7 Partnerships — borrow other people's trust

- **Estate/letting agents as channel partners** (overlaps 1.4 but worth its own line): the goal is to become the named "we use these guys" maintenance firm an agent recommends to landlords. That's a referral pipe that runs for years.
- **Property-adjacent businesses:** local accountants and solicitors with landlord clients, BTL mortgage brokers, inventory clerks, EPC assessors. They meet your exact buyer at the exact moment maintenance comes up.
- **Suppliers:** the local builders' merchant / decorator's centre — sometimes a noticeboard, sometimes a referral, sometimes trade-day visibility.
- **Community:** sponsor something small and local (a school fair banner, a community-centre noticeboard, a local sports team kit). Cheap, genuine, and it puts the brand in front of homeowners and small commercial sites in the patch. Don't overspend — visibility, not vanity.

### 1.8 Social & light brand presence — support act, not headliner

Social won't be the lead engine, but it's a cheap trust and "are they real / are they active" check.

- **Facebook + Instagram + a local Nextdoor presence.** Post 2–3×/week: before/afters, a finished job, the team, a quick tip. People hire trades they can *see* doing good work.
- Join and helpfully participate in **local community / landlord Facebook groups** (Lewisham, Greenwich, etc.) — answer questions, don't spam. Recommendations in those groups convert.
- **LinkedIn** for the B2B side: connect with local agents, block managers, FMs; post the occasional B2B proof piece. Low effort, keeps you visible to the decision-makers.
- Branding stays "Stone & Ember" everywhere — near-black, the scarce ember accent, warm cream, the wordmark, the tagline. Consistency is a trust signal.

## 2. The 90-day launch plan

The first quarter is about laying foundations that compound (GBP, reviews, the B2B list) and getting *some* leads in the door fast (ads, directories, referrals) so there's cashflow and proof to point at. Don't try to do all eight channels at once — sequence them.

### Days 1–30 — Foundations & quick wins
**Theme: get findable, get the first leads, start the list.**
- Claim + fully complete Google Business Profile. Upload 15–20 real job photos. Set categories/services/area properly.
- Apply for Google Local Services Ads (start the Google Guaranteed verification — it takes time).
- Get the website's trade-area pages live for the **top 2 trades × top 2 areas** (e.g. carpentry-Lewisham, painting-Greenwich) + LocalBusiness schema + NAP.
- Set up conversion tracking (calls + forms) before any ad spend.
- Build the **review-request flow**: QR + one-tap link on the completion email/invoice. Ask every customer from day one.
- Start the **B2B list** — first 30 local agents identified and in the CRM.
- Set up Facebook/Instagram, post the first few jobs.
- Free citations: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yell — consistent NAP.
- Sign up to **one** pay-per-lead platform (Bark or MyBuilder) for early cashflow + first reviews.

**End-of-month target:** GBP live, 5+ reviews, 8–12 leads (mostly ads/directories/referral), 30 B2B contacts logged.

### Days 31–60 — Outreach engine on, ads tuned
**Theme: start the B2B sequence properly; make ads efficient.**
- Print the B2B one-pager. Begin **hand-delivered drop-ins** to local agent offices — 8–12/week.
- Launch the follow-up email + trial-job offer sequence.
- LSA live (once verified); launch tight Search Ads on top trades. Watch cost-per-lead daily for the first fortnight, prune negatives, kill losers.
- Publish the first 2 content pieces (1 homeowner guide + 1 B2B/wedge piece).
- Add the next 2 trade-area pages.
- Keep the review flywheel turning — push toward 15+ total.
- Decide on Checkatrade (join if the homeowner pipeline justifies it).

**End-of-month target:** 15–25 leads, 15+ reviews, B2B list at 60+, first trial job(s) booked with an agent.

### Days 61–90 — Convert & systematise
**Theme: turn trials into recurring; lock in the weekly rhythm.**
- Convert trial jobs into **standing/preferred-supplier arrangements** — aim for the **first 1–2 recurring B2B accounts**.
- First monthly B2B nurture email to the whole list.
- Scale whatever's working in ads; cut whatever isn't.
- Content: 2 more pieces. Trade-area pages now cover all 4 trades × top areas.
- First trade cross-referral relationships in place (a gas engineer, an electrician).
- Review the numbers against KPIs (section 6); write down what's working and reallocate next quarter's budget accordingly.

**End-of-quarter target:** 25–35 leads/month run-rate, 25+ reviews, **1–2 recurring B2B accounts signed**, repeatable weekly rhythm established.

## 3. Steady-state (months 4–12)

Once the launch quarter's done, the job changes from *building* to *compounding and converting*. The machine is: GBP + reviews + content pull leads in organically (cost falls), the B2B sequence runs every week converting trials to contracts, ads fill the gaps, referrals snowball.

- **Lead mix shifts** from paid (ads/directories) toward organic (GBP/SEO/referral) as ranking and reputation build — which is what lets the budget ramp *down* per-lead even as volume ramps *up*.
- **B2B conversion is the monthly priority** — every month, X new contacts into the sequence, trials won, trials converted to recurring. This is the number that hits the 6–10 accounts and the 30% recurring-revenue goal.
- **Reviews never stop** — keep asking every job; coast past 50.
- **Double down on winners, cut dead weight** — by month 6 you'll know which channels earn their keep. Stop funding the ones that don't (likely some pay-per-lead platforms) and pour it into what works (almost certainly GBP/local + B2B + the best-converting ad groups).
- **Seasonal pushes** ride the calendar (section 4) — void-prep, pre-winter, pre-Christmas commercial.

## 6. Weekly marketing operating rhythm

One operator, marketing fitted around the day job. The point of a fixed rhythm is that marketing *happens* even in a busy week, instead of being the thing that always slips. Roughly 4–5 focused hours/week.

| When | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday (15 min)** | Check last week's numbers (leads, source, calls, ad cost-per-lead). Glance at the funnel. Pick the week's one priority. | 15m |
| **Monday (30 min)** | Add 8–12 new B2B contacts to the CRM + sequence. Send the week's follow-up emails to warm agents. | 30m |
| **Tuesday (45 min)** | B2B drop-ins — hand-deliver one-pagers to 4–6 agent offices on the way to/from a job. | en route |
| **Wednesday (45 min)** | Content: write/finish one piece this fortnight; one Google Post; schedule 2–3 social posts. | 45m |
| **Thursday (20 min)** | Reviews: chase every signed-off job from the week for a Google review. Reply to any new reviews. Add fresh job photos to GBP. | 20m |
| **Friday (20 min)** | Ads check: prune negatives, dispute junk LSA leads, note what converted. Respond to any inbound leads in the pipeline. | 20m |
| **Last Friday of month (60 min)** | Monthly review: KPIs vs targets, send the monthly B2B nurture email, reallocate budget, set next month's focus. | 60m |

Non-negotiables that must happen *every* week regardless: ask for reviews, log lead sources, add B2B contacts, respond to inbound within hours (not days — speed-to-lead wins jobs).

## 7. Lead funnel + KPIs

**The funnel, stage by stage:**

```
Discovery        → GBP / Ads / referral / directory / drop-in / content
  ↓
Enquiry          → call to 07539 705576, form fill, or email to hello@
  ↓
Quote            → fixed price agreed IN WRITING (the wedge)
  ↓
Job won          → scheduled in the app
  ↓
On-site & done   → live tracking link, tidy finish, signed off
  ↓
Invoiced & paid  → itemised VAT invoice, bank transfer, per-property record
  ↓
Review + repeat  → review requested; homeowner → referral, B2B → recurring
```

The whole model leans on two conversion moments: **enquiry → written quote** (where the "here's the receipt" pitch beats the cheaper sole trader) and, for B2B, **trial job → standing arrangement** (where the record-keeping converts a one-off into recurring revenue). Everything upstream feeds those; everything downstream compounds them.

**KPIs and targets — track monthly:**

| Metric | What it tells you | M3 target | M6 target | M12 target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified leads / month | Top-of-funnel health | 20–25 | 30–40 | **40–60** |
| Lead source split | Which channels earn their keep | tracked | organic rising | organic-led |
| Enquiry → quote rate | Are we getting to a price? | 60%+ | 65%+ | 70%+ |
| Quote → won rate | Is the pitch landing? | 30%+ | 35%+ | 40%+ |
| Cost per lead (paid) | Ad/directory efficiency | baseline | falling | < baseline |
| Cost per acquired job | True paid efficiency | baseline | falling | falling |
| New Google reviews / month | Reputation moat | 4–5 | 4–5 | 4–5 (50+ total) |
| Total Google reviews | The moat itself | 25+ | 35+ | **50+** |
| Map Pack position (priority terms) | Local SEO win | improving | top 5 | **top 3** |
| B2B contacts in sequence | Outreach activity | 60+ | 120+ | 200+ |
| Trial jobs won (B2B) | Door-opening | 2–3 | 6+ | 12+ |
| **Recurring B2B accounts signed** | The growth prize | 1–2 | 3–5 | **6–10** |
| % revenue recurring/contracted | Business resilience | 10%+ | 20%+ | **30%+** |

Two leading indicators worth watching weekly because they predict everything else: **inbound response time** (answer fast or lose the job) and **B2B drop-ins + follow-ups completed** (the activity that becomes next quarter's recurring revenue).
